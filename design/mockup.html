Here's the strange part: serious people have predicted a US debt crisis since 1981, and they've been wrong for forty years straight. Either they're all fools, or they're early. Want to figure out which?

ok yes. why have they been wrong?
Guide · Turn 3

About $7 trillion is owed to other parts of the US government itself — Social Security's trust fund, mostly. Another $8 trillion sits with the Federal Reserve, domestic banks, and pension funds. Foreign governments hold roughly $7.5 trillion, with Japan ($1.1T) and China ($0.8T) as the two largest creditors — both of whom need dollar-denominated assets to run their own economies.

So the actual picture: most of the debt is the US owing itself, and the largest foreign holders are allies with strong reasons to stay in. Which raises the obvious next question — if it's so circular, what part of it actually matters?

ok but that just sounds like it's fine forever, which can't be right
Guide · Turn 5

You're right to push on that — but "fine forever" is smuggling in an assumption. "The US owes itself" is true only as long as interest rates stay low. At 2%, the annual interest payment is roughly $700B — heavy but manageable. At 5%, it's $1.75 trillion, which is more than the military and Medicare combined. The size of the debt is almost beside the point. What matters is the rate the market charges to hold it — and that can move fast.
